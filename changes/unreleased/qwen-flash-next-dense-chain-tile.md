## New features and improvements

- The tiled prefill's dense EXL3 linears (the chain GEMM: Qwen3.8-Flash-Next's linear-attention and full-attention projections, and the other models' prefill linears from 3 bits up) run through a 128 x 128 tile that two CTAs share an SM with, instead of a 64 x 128 or 128 x 256 tile that took the SM's shared memory for one CTA at 6 to 8 bits. One CTA's epilogue and pipeline fill now hide under the other's tensor-core work. Each output's K sum is still one chain of m16n8k16 MMAs over the k tiles in order from +0.0 with the same decoder and epilogue, so every output bit is unchanged: a test compares the new kernel with the old one bit for bit at 1 to 4,096 rows, with a bias, for three output types, on odd strip counts, at 3 to 8 bits and on adversarial rows, and the 9 pinned replies of the benchmark, drafted == serial and chunked (2,048 rows) == single-chunk prefill still match. Measured on one GB10 locked at 2,200 MHz, Flash-Next, 4,096-row chunks: the dense GEMM takes 370 ms per chunk before and 280 ms after (nsys), and a cold 32K prompt prefills at 1,641 tok/s before and 1,695 to 1,705 tok/s after (8K: 1,612 to 1,664 to 1,671). Per call at 4,096 rows: in_proj_qkv 3.65 to 2.82 ms, in_proj_z 2.21 to 1.76 ms, out_proj 2.52 to 2.10 ms, q_proj 4.21 to 3.31 ms; a 4-bit 5,120 x 17,408 layer 9.64 to 8.75 ms. Chunks below 1,024 rows (below 512 for a layer with 2,048 columns or more) keep the 64-row tiles, which are faster there (a 512-column layer at 512 rows: 47 against 74 us).
